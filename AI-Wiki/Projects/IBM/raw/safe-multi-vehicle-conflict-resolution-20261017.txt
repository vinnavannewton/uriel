# URIEL — Safe Multi-Vehicle Conflict Resolution

## Objective

Implement a conflict-resolution controller that handles two or more vehicles approaching or occupying a shared intersection conflict zone. URIEL must assess stopping feasibility, protect vehicles already inside the conflict zone, select a safe crossing order, and verify clearance before releasing conflicting traffic.

**Critical rule:** Never command every vehicle to stop unconditionally when a conflict is detected. A vehicle may already be inside the conflict zone or may be unable to stop safely within the available distance.

## 1. Required Solution: State-Aware Conflict Response

When a potential conflict is detected, URIEL must execute the following process:

### Step 1 — Observe and validate

Collect the latest available data for every relevant vehicle:
- Vehicle ID, position, orientation and speed.
- Velocity and acceleration.
- Route and predicted trajectory.
- Distance to the conflict zone.
- Estimated time to entry and rear clearance.
- Vehicle dimensions and occupied footprint.
- Braking capability and estimated stopping distance.
- Telemetry freshness and prediction uncertainty.
- Current conflict-zone occupancy.
- LiDAR observations, where available.

Use simulator ground truth as the primary source of vehicle state during controlled BeamNG experiments. Treat LiDAR as a perception input and cross-check detections against ground truth.

If critical measurements are stale, missing or inconsistent, mark the situation uncertain and prevent new conflicting reservations.

### Step 2 — Classify each vehicle

Assign each vehicle one of these states:

- `APPROACHING`: Approaching the conflict zone.
- `STOPPING_FEASIBLE`: Can be expected to stop before the conflict zone within the validated braking envelope.
- `STOPPING_UNCERTAIN`: Available data does not establish whether stopping is feasible.
- `INSIDE_CONFLICT_ZONE`: Vehicle footprint occupies the conflict zone.
- `CLEARING_CONFLICT_ZONE`: Vehicle is moving through the zone and has not fully cleared it.
- `STOPPED_BEFORE_ZONE`: Vehicle is confirmed stopped before entry.
- `CLEARED`: Vehicle's rear boundary has passed beyond the relevant conflict zone.
- `STATE_UNKNOWN`: Vehicle state cannot be verified reliably.

A vehicle's state must be updated from observations, not inferred solely from the command previously sent to it.

### Step 3 — Estimate stopping feasibility

For each approaching vehicle, estimate whether it can stop before the conflict zone.

Use measured or validated braking performance for the vehicle, road gradient, surface friction, loading, current speed and available distance.

A simplified preliminary estimate is:

`stopping_distance = reaction_distance + braking_distance + safety_margin`

Under a simplified constant-deceleration model:

`braking_distance = speed² / (2 × deceleration)`

This estimate is not sufficient on its own. Include control delay, braking response, slope, uncertainty and the vehicle's occupied footprint. Use validated simulation measurements to establish the actual stopping envelope.

Classify stopping as feasible only when the available distance exceeds the conservative estimated stopping distance and the assumptions are valid.

If stopping feasibility is uncertain, do not issue an unverified emergency-braking command and assume the vehicle will stop safely. Apply the configured conservative response and continue evaluating the available safe options.

### Step 4 — Apply state-aware actions

**Case A: Vehicle is already inside the conflict zone**

- Do not automatically stop it in the middle of the intersection.
- Preserve its ability to clear the zone when continuing is verified safer than stopping.
- Prevent incompatible vehicles from entering its occupied or predicted swept path.
- Track its trajectory until rear clearance is verified.
- If it is obstructed or cannot safely continue, enter `SAFE_HOLD` and apply the validated fallback policy.

**Case B: Vehicle is approaching and can stop safely**

- Issue a yield or stop command through the verified vehicle-control interface.
- Confirm that the intended vehicle received the command.
- Measure its actual speed and braking response.
- Mark it `STOPPED_BEFORE_ZONE` only after the stopping condition is verified.
- Keep it waiting until a valid reservation is granted.

**Case C: Vehicle may be unable to stop safely**

- Do not blindly apply a full stop command.
- Evaluate its current trajectory and the available safe actions.
- Prevent other vehicles from entering the conflicting path.
- If the vehicle is already committed to crossing, allow clearance only when the controller verifies that doing so is the safer available option.
- If no safe action can be established, enter `SAFE_HOLD`, block new conflicting releases and log the unresolved hazard.

**Case D: Vehicle state is unknown**

- Do not assume that the vehicle is stopped, absent or clear.
- Block new reservations that could conflict with its possible occupancy.
- Attempt to recover valid telemetry.
- Resume coordination only after the relevant state is verified or the validated fallback procedure establishes a safe condition.

### Step 5 — Determine the crossing order

Once the conflict has been contained, evaluate eligible vehicles for the next crossing opportunity.

Consider:
- Whether the vehicle is already occupying the zone.
- Whether it can stop safely.
- Whether its route conflicts with other vehicles.
- Predicted entry and rear-clearance times.
- Prediction uncertainty.
- Whether the path can be kept clear for the complete vehicle footprint.
- Waiting time and fairness.
- Whether its movement could create a new conflict elsewhere.

Select one eligible vehicle or one explicitly validated group of non-conflicting vehicles.

For the initial implementation, use **one vehicle at a time through a shared conflict zone**. Concurrent movement may be enabled later only when geometry and trajectory checks demonstrate that the vehicles' occupied paths do not conflict.

Do not simply select the vehicle with the shortest predicted arrival time.

### Step 6 — Reserve, release and verify

Implement the following state transitions:

1. `FREE`: No conflicting occupancy or reservation exists.
2. `CONFLICT_DETECTED`: Potentially incompatible movements are identified.
3. `ASSESSING`: Vehicle states, stopping feasibility and occupancy are evaluated.
4. `HOLD`: Eligible approaching vehicles are instructed to yield or stop.
5. `RESERVED`: One eligible vehicle receives a crossing reservation.
6. `CROSSING`: The selected vehicle enters or occupies the conflict zone.
7. `CLEARANCE_CHECK`: The controller verifies its rear clearance and checks for new conflicts.
8. `RELEASE_NEXT`: The next eligible vehicle is evaluated.
9. `SAFE_HOLD`: The controller cannot establish a safe release condition.

Do not transition to `RELEASE_NEXT` merely because an estimated clearance time has expired. Verify actual rear clearance and account for sensor uncertainty, telemetry delay and other vehicles' current states.

If a vehicle remains inside the zone, loses communication, deviates from its route or fails to clear it, retain the reservation and block incompatible movements.

## 2. Independent Safety Monitor

Implement a separate safety monitor that checks every proposed controller action before it is issued.

The monitor must reject a release when:
- Another vehicle occupies the same conflict zone.
- Predicted vehicle footprints overlap in space and time.
- Stopping feasibility or clearance cannot be established where required.
- Critical telemetry is stale or invalid.
- The reservation state is inconsistent.
- The intended vehicle identity cannot be verified.

The monitor must also avoid assuming that stopping is always the safest action. Evaluate the vehicle's current motion, stopping envelope and zone occupancy before accepting a stop command.

If the controller and safety monitor disagree, do not release a new conflicting vehicle. Preserve existing safe clearance actions where justified by the validated policy and log the disagreement.

## 3. LiDAR and Ground-Truth Handling

For the red and blue subject vehicles:
- Acquire LiDAR readings with simulation timestamps.
- Detect nearby objects and potential obstacles.
- Track sensor pose and coordinate frame.
- Compare detections with simulator ground truth during validation.
- Record missing or invalid sensor readings.
- Avoid treating an unobserved object as proof that the path is clear.

LiDAR detection alone does not establish a vehicle's braking capability or guarantee safe clearance. Combine perception with validated simulator state, motion estimates and uncertainty checks.

## 4. Command and Response Verification

For every stop, yield, resume or speed command, record:
- Command ID and timestamp.
- Target vehicle ID.
- Command type and parameters.
- Reservation and controller state at issuance.
- Command acknowledgement, if supported.
- Subsequent vehicle speed, position and control response.
- Whether the expected response was observed.
- Timeout or failure details.

Never mark a vehicle stopped solely because a command was sent. Never mark a vehicle cleared solely because its reservation timer expired.

If a command fails or its effect cannot be verified, prevent incompatible releases and invoke the defined fallback.

## 5. Test Scenarios

Run controlled simulation tests for:

1. Two approaching vehicles with sufficient stopping distance.
2. Two approaching vehicles where one has limited stopping distance.
3. A vehicle already occupying the conflict zone.
4. Three or more vehicles approaching different conflict paths.
5. A vehicle stopping before entry.
6. A vehicle that never arrives.
7. A vehicle that stops or becomes obstructed inside the zone.
8. Stale telemetry or a lost command acknowledgement.
9. LiDAR temporarily failing to detect a relevant obstacle.
10. A vehicle deviating from its assigned route.

Use repeatable starting conditions and recorded random seeds. Do not force physical collisions to create test data; create controlled near-conflict conditions and measure the actual outcomes.

## 6. Acceptance Criteria

A trial passes the controller's basic validation only if:
- Vehicle identities and relevant states are verified.
- No incompatible reservation is granted.
- Vehicles already inside the zone are handled according to the validated clearance policy.
- Stop commands are based on stopping-feasibility assessment.
- Actual stopping responses are measured.
- The next vehicle is released only after the required conflict-zone clearance checks pass.
- Uncertain states trigger the defined conservative fallback.
- All decisions, commands, responses and outcomes are recorded.

Report failures and unresolved states explicitly. Three successful trials do not establish general collision-avoidance safety.

## 7. Implementation Deliverables

Produce:
- Stopping-feasibility estimator.
- Multi-vehicle conflict detector.
- Vehicle-state classifier.
- Conflict-zone reservation manager.
- Independent safety monitor.
- Verified command/response logger.
- LiDAR and ground-truth validation.
- Controlled test scenarios.
- Trial reports and failure analysis.

Inspect the existing BeamNG implementation before changing code. Preserve previous data, verify API support, and never fabricate successful safety outcomes.
